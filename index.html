§ 01   Aboutنبذة

A Yemen researcher and MENA analyst working where evidence meets humanitarian practice.

Currently: Ph.D. candidate in Political Science at Western University, offering independent consultations on Yemen and the MENA region.

Hayl Al-Salehi is a Yemen researcher, MENA analyst, and crisis analyst based at Western University in London, Ontario. From 2020 to 2025 he worked as a Crisis Analyst with Mercy Corps, producing conflict analysis and early-warning research that informed humanitarian programming in Yemen and across the Middle East and North Africa.

As a quantitative researcher, Hayl works in R, Python, and Stata, applying survey experiments, statistical modeling, and geospatial analysis to questions of civil war, political violence, and post-conflict reintegration. His doctoral research examines rebel diplomacy — when and why armed non-state actors pursue negotiation rather than escalation — combining global conflict datasets with a two-country survey experiment.

His humanitarian and policy work has spanned roles at Mercy Corps, the Yemen Policy Center, and the Centre for Information Resilience, with field research conducted in Yemen and Lebanon. A native Arabic speaker, he supports humanitarian organizations, think tanks, and media with crisis analysis, Yemen and MENA political analysis, and evidence for program design in fragile and conflict-affected settings.

Hayl is available for collaboration, consultation, and media commentary as a Yemen analyst and MENA researcher, including short-term engagements with NGOs, policy organizations, newsrooms, and academic teams.

§ 03   Researchالبحث

A dissertation in three papers, plus collaborative work on political violence, the Yemen conflict, and authoritarian politics across the MENA region.

Below: the three papers that make up the dissertation. A full publications list, including peer-reviewed work and earlier collaborations, is on the CV.
i. Paper 01 · Under Review

Diplomats or Dead Ends? Rebel Diplomacy, Survival, and Success in Civil Wars

Tests whether diplomatic engagement extends rebel groups' organizational lifespan or accelerates their collapse, using a global dataset of armed non-state actors.

Solo-authored
ii. Paper 02 · In Progress

When Do Rebels Build Embassies?

Examines how organizational capacity and external support shape the choice between diplomacy and military escalation among insurgent groups.

With Blair Welsh
iii. Paper 03 · In Progress

Talking to Rebels? Public Attitudes Toward Rebel Diplomacy

A two-country survey experiment in the United States and Nigeria measuring how citizens evaluate negotiations with armed non-state actors.

Solo-authored
